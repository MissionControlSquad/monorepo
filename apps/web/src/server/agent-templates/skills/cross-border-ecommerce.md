### Customs and Trade Compliance

**Import classification (HS codes):**
- Every product requires an HS (Harmonized System) code — the international standard for customs classification
- Incorrect HS codes cause customs delays, additional duties, and potential fines
- Use the World Customs Organization's HS lookup or your freight forwarder for classification
- Maintain a product tariff code library; update when regulations change

**Common compliance requirements by product category:**
- **Electronics:** CE marking (EU), FCC certification (US), PSE (Japan), KC (South Korea)
- **Food/supplements:** FSMA registration (US), EFSA compliance (EU), SFDA/NMPA (China), TGA (Australia)
- **Cosmetics:** CPSR (EU), FDA registration (US), NMPA (China), KFDA (South Korea)
- **Children's products:** ASTM (US), EN71 (EU) — strictest compliance requirements; non-compliance is catastrophic for brand

**DDP (Delivered Duty Paid) vs. DDU (Delivered Duty Unpaid):**
- **DDP:** Seller pays all import duties and taxes — customer receives package with no surprise costs. Highest conversion; required for competitive markets like Germany and Japan
- **DDU/DAP:** Buyer pays duties on delivery — causes package refusals, negative reviews, and chargebacks; acceptable in some markets (US imports from outside) but increasingly a conversion liability

**De minimis thresholds (duty-free import limits):**
- US: $800 per shipment (highest in the world — enables low-cost CBEC to US)
- EU: €150 (reduced from €22 in 2021; VAT always applies for B2C)
- China: 5,000 RMB per transaction (CBEC policy)
- Australia: AUD $1,000

### Localization Strategy

**Localization beyond translation:**
Translation is the minimum. Full localization includes:
1. **Language:** Native-speaker translation, not machine translation; idioms, product descriptions, customer service
2. **Currency:** Local currency display AND local currency settlement (not just display)
3. **Sizing/units:** Clothing sizes, weight units, voltage — must match local standards
4. **Cultural adaptation:** Color symbolism, imagery, holidays (Red in China = luck; Red in South Africa = mourning)
5. **Payment methods:** Missing the dominant local payment method costs 15–30% of potential orders
6. **Trust signals:** Local phone number, local return address, local review platform integration

**Localization priority hierarchy:**
- Must have: Language, currency, local payment method, correct product certifications
- Should have: Local customer service hours, local social proof, cultural image adaptation
- Nice to have: Local domain (ccTLD), local warehouse for fast shipping, local loyalty program integration

**FX (foreign exchange) risk management:**
- Price in local currency; hedge FX exposure on a monthly basis
- At <$100K/month international revenue: accept FX risk as cost of doing business
- At >$100K/month: Open local currency accounts (Wise Business, Mercury) to receive and hold local currency before converting
- Review pricing in each currency quarterly — FX rate shifts can make prices uncompetitive or excessively high without a pricing audit

### International Logistics and Fulfillment

**Fulfillment model options:**

1. **Cross-border shipping (domestic warehouse → international customer):**
   - Pros: Single inventory; no upfront local investment
   - Cons: 7–21 day delivery; higher shipping cost; customs per-package risk
   - Best for: Testing markets; high-value products where delivery speed is less critical

2. **Regional 3PL (Third-party logistics warehouse in target region):**
   - Pros: 1–5 day local delivery; lower per-unit shipping cost at scale; avoids customs per package
   - Cons: Inventory split; minimum volume requirements; local import compliance required
   - Best for: Established markets with $50K+/month revenue

3. **Amazon FBA International:**
   - Pros: Prime shipping eligibility; Amazon handles customs for Amazon Global; no 3PL relationship
   - Cons: Amazon fees; pricing transparency to competitors; inventory risk
   - Best for: Product categories where Amazon dominates the market

**Carrier selection by region:**
- US: UPS, FedEx, USPS for domestic; DHL for cross-border
- Europe: DPD, DHL Parcel, Hermes (Evri UK) for domestic; DHL Express cross-border
- Asia-Pacific: DHL, FedEx, SF Express; local: J&T, Flash Express in SEA
- Last-mile in China: 顺丰 (SF Express) is premium; 中通/圆通 for cost efficiency

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Assuming domestic pricing translates internationally:** International pricing must account for duties, shipping, FX, local competitive landscape, and local purchasing power parity — a US $50 product may need to be £55 in the UK, not £40 (simple conversion)
- **Launching in too many markets simultaneously:** Two markets done well outperform six markets done poorly — prove the model in 1–2 markets before international expansion
- **Underestimating return logistics:** International returns are logistically complex and expensive; a clear, affordable return policy is required for conversion in Germany and UK; budget 5–15% of revenue for returns processing costs
- **Ignoring local payment methods:** Missing PayNow in Singapore, SEPA bank transfer in Germany, Pix in Brazil, or GrabPay in SEA causes significant conversion loss — payment localization is not optional
- **Using machine translation for product pages:** Machine-translated product descriptions in German, Japanese, or Chinese are obvious to native speakers and severely damage trust; professional native-speaker translation is a non-negotiable investment
- **No local customer service capability:** Cross-border customers expect service in their language and time zone; routing all support through a single English-language center loses 30–40% of resolution success

## Success Metrics

- **International revenue as % of total:** Target 15–30% of total revenue from international markets within 12 months of active multi-market expansion
- **Cross-border conversion rate:** Within 20% of domestic conversion rate in each market within 6 months (gap narrows as localization improves)
- **Customs clearance success rate:** 98%+ of international shipments clearing customs without delays or additional fees
- **International NPS:** Net Promoter Score within 10 points of domestic NPS (measures localization quality and service parity)
- **Average delivery time vs. target:** 90%+ of orders delivered within the published delivery window in each market
