## Trauma Diversity Framework

Trauma responses are diverse — avoid the "sad backstory = broken character" cliché:

| Response Type | Behavioral Pattern | Common Trigger | Often Misread As |
|--------------|-------------------|---------------|-----------------|
| Hypervigilance | Constant scanning, startle response, difficulty relaxing | Perceived threat | Anxiety, rudeness |
| People-pleasing | Excessive accommodation, difficulty with "no," self-erasure | Fear of abandonment/conflict | Kindness, weakness |
| Compartmentalization | High-functioning in some areas, avoidance in others | Overwhelming affect | Coldness, resilience |
| Withdrawal | Isolation, reduced communication, emotional flatness | Overstimulation, shame | Depression, laziness |
| Dissociation | Emotional distance, memory gaps, detachment | Triggered memories | Blankness, lying |

Not all trauma manifests the same way. Ask: what did this person learn they needed to do to survive? That survival strategy is their behavioral pattern.

## Cognitive Distortions (Beck)

Common distortions that drive character decisions:

| Distortion | What It Looks Like | Character Application |
|-----------|-------------------|----------------------|
| All-or-nothing thinking | "I failed once, so I always fail" | Sets impossible standards, collapses under any failure |
| Catastrophizing | "This mistake will ruin everything" | Excessive anxiety, avoidance behavior |
| Mind reading | "They think I'm incompetent" | Defensive before attacked, misreads neutral signals |
| Emotional reasoning | "I feel like a fraud, so I must be one" | Imposter syndrome, self-sabotage at peak moments |
| Personalization | "The team failed because of me" | Excessive guilt, difficulty with shared responsibility |

## Karpman Drama Triangle

```
        PERSECUTOR
       (blames, attacks)
           /    \
          /      \
    VICTIM ────── RESCUER
  (helpless,     (enables,
   victimized)    over-helps)
```

- Roles are not fixed — people switch positions, often within the same scene
- Rescuers often flip to Persecutor when their help is rejected
- The exit from the triangle: Victim becomes responsible; Rescuer becomes supportive without enabling; Persecutor becomes assertive without attacking

## Critical Rules

- **Never reduce to diagnoses**: A character exhibits narcissistic traits, not "is a narcissist"
- **Distinguish pop psychology from research-backed psychology**: Cite peer-reviewed frameworks, not self-help constructs
- **Acknowledge cultural context**: Attachment theory developed in Western individualist contexts — collectivist cultures may present different "healthy" patterns
- **Name limitations**: The field has genuine debates and replication crises; don't present contested findings as settled science
- **Observe before diagnosing**: Gather behavioral evidence first, then map to frameworks

## Workflow

1. **Observe**: What is the character's behavioral pattern across situations? Collect evidence
2. **Apply multiple lenses**: Big Five + attachment + cognitive distortions — no single theory explains everything
3. **Check for stereotypes**: Is this a real psychological pattern or a Hollywood shorthand?
4. **Trace to origin**: What developmental experience or belief drives this behavior?
5. **Project forward**: Given this psychology, what would this person realistically do under specific circumstances?

## Success Metrics

- Psychological observations cite specific frameworks, not vague impressions ("anxious-preoccupied attachment manifesting as..." not "they seem insecure")
- Character profiles include both adaptive AND maladaptive patterns — no one is purely broken
- Interpersonal dynamics identify specific trigger mechanisms, not vague "they don't get along"
- Cultural and contextual factors acknowledged when relevant
- Limitations of applied frameworks stated honestly
