## Core Methodology

### Understanding Chaebol Dynamics

**The Chaebol Ecosystem:**
Korea's economy is dominated by large family-controlled conglomerates:
- Samsung Group (electronics, construction, insurance, biotech)
- Hyundai (automotive, heavy industry, construction)
- SK Group (energy, telecom, semiconductors)
- LG (electronics, chemicals, telecom)
- Lotte (retail, food, hospitality, chemicals)
- Kakao / Naver (tech-era conglomerates)

**Working with Chaebols:**
- Decision-making is top-down; working-level employees present options to senior management, who decide
- Long approval chains — don't mistake slow response for disinterest; the decision is being escalated appropriately
- Loyalty to the group and hierarchy is paramount; never approach a working-level contact with a request that bypasses their superiors
- Annual procurement cycles: major purchasing decisions tied to fiscal year (April or January depending on group)
- Preferred vendor status matters enormously — getting on the approved vendor list takes time but enables recurring business

**SMB Landscape:**
- Chaebol suppliers and subcontractors (협력사, hyeopryeoksa): deeply embedded in chaebol supply chains; relationships are semi-permanent
- Korean SMBs increasingly export via Coupang Rocket, G-market, and global platforms
- Startup ecosystem: Seoul's Gangnam (Teheran-ro corridor), Pangyo Techno Valley — Korea's equivalent of Silicon Valley

### Korean Business Etiquette

**Hierarchy and Titles:**
- Always use full title: "부장님 (Deputy General Manager)" not first name
- Business cards: receive with both hands, read carefully, place respectfully on the table (never pocket immediately)
- Seating: senior person sits farthest from the door; always wait for host to indicate seating
- Age matters: even among peers, the older person is senior in social hierarchy
- Learn basic Korean business titles: 대리 (Daeri, assistant manager), 과장 (Gwajang, section chief), 차장 (Chajang, deputy general manager), 부장 (Bujang, general manager), 이사 (Isa, director), 상무 (Sangmu, managing director)

**Kibun and Nunchi:**
- **Kibun** (기분): emotional atmosphere/mood — preserving kibun means keeping the atmosphere harmonious; never cause someone to lose face publicly
- **Nunchi** (눈치): reading unspoken signals and adjusting behavior accordingly — a high-nunchi person understands what is not said; foreigners who lack nunchi are seen as socially obtuse
- Criticism: always private, never public; frame as suggestion, not criticism
- Rejection: Korean "yes" often means "I heard you," not agreement. "We'll think about it" often means no. Learn to read indirect signals.

**Meeting Protocol:**
- Pre-meeting preparation: understand the org chart; know who the senior person is
- Opening: greetings, exchange of business cards, light small talk — do not rush to agenda
- Presentation: detailed, visually polished; PowerPoint-heavy culture; show you've done your research on their company
- Closing: summarize next steps clearly; Koreans prefer explicit action items with named owners
- After-meeting dinners (회식, hoesik): critically important for relationship building; declining shows disinterest; eating and drinking together is how trust is established

**Gift-Giving:**
- Appropriate for first meetings and Korean holidays (Chuseok in autumn, Lunar New Year)
- Appropriate gifts: premium Korean liquor (한국 전통주), quality fruit sets, premium food items, golf accessories (golf is Korea's prestige sport)
- Presentation matters: wrapped gifts, both hands
- Gifts from your home country carry novelty value
- Do not give: sharp objects (imply severing relationship), red ink (associated with death), shoes (connotation of "walking away"), sets of 4 (tetraphobia — 4 = death in Korean)

### Korean Digital Platforms

**Naver:**
- ~70% search market share in Korea (Google is secondary)
- Naver Blog and Café heavily influence purchase decisions — more than Google-indexed content
- Naver Smart Store: Korea's dominant product discovery and commerce platform
- Naver SEO key factors: Naver Blog posts, Naver Café participation, Naver Shopping listings, Naver Map for local businesses
- Naver Advertising: CPC search ads (SA), display ads via GFA (Naver's GDN equivalent), Naver Shopping Ads

**Kakao Ecosystem:**
- KakaoTalk: ~95% of Korean smartphone users — the primary messaging app
- Kakao Channel (카카오채널): business pages within KakaoTalk — more effective than email marketing for Korea
- Kakao Pay: dominant mobile payment (along with NaverPay, Samsung Pay)
- Kakao Commerce: Kakao Gift (선물하기) — gifting feature used for corporate gifts and B2C
- KakaoMap: second to Naver Map but important; business listings required

**Coupang:**
- Amazon equivalent; Rocket Delivery (rocketdelivery) = prime-equivalent; Korean consumers expect next-day delivery
- Coupang Rocket Jik-gu: cross-border commerce from global brands
- Seller registration: requires Korean business registration number or Coupang Global Seller Program for non-Korean entities

**Payment Preferences:**
- Korean consumers: KakaoPay, NaverPay, Samsung Pay, credit cards (mainly Shinhan, KB Kookmin, Hyundai Card)
- B2B: electronic funds transfer to Korean bank accounts; Kakao Pay for Business
- Foreign payment methods (PayPal, international Stripe) have low adoption in domestic B2C

### Korean Regulatory Environment

**PIPA (Personal Information Protection Act):**
- Stricter than GDPR in several respects for sensitive data
- Mandatory privacy impact assessment (PIA) for large-scale personal data processing
- Cross-border data transfers: require consent or PIPC approval mechanism
- Data localization consideration: financial and health data — specific sector rules apply

**K-ISMS (Korean Information Security Management System):**
- Mandatory certification for ISPs, IDC operators, hospitals, and large online services
- ISMS-P: integrated version covering privacy management

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

- **Treating Korea like China or Japan:** Korea shares Confucian roots with both neighbors but has distinct business culture. Ppalli ppalli (빨리빨리 — "hurry hurry") culture means faster decision cycles than Japan; more direct communication than China.
- **Skipping the hoesik:** Declining after-work dinner invitations signals disengagement. In Korean culture, the relationship built over food and drink is what enables the business conversation; the meeting room is just for documentation.
- **Addressing senior contacts by first name:** Even in international companies operating in Korea, Korean colleagues use titles. Defaulting to first names without permission signals cultural ignorance.
- **Ignoring Naver for digital marketing:** Assuming Google-optimized SEO works in Korea. Content that ranks on Google is often invisible on Naver. Build Naver Blog presence and Naver Shopping listings separately.
- **Assuming one chaebol contact covers the group:** Samsung Electronics contacts do not give you Samsung SDS or Samsung Heavy Industries. Each subsidiary has its own procurement organization. Map each entity separately.
