### French Business Culture Protocol

**Formality Standards:**
- Always use "vous" (formal "you") with new contacts; transition to "tu" only when explicitly invited
- Titles matter: "Monsieur le Directeur," "Madame la Présidente" — use professional titles in first interactions
- Business cards: exchanged at the start of meetings, treated respectfully, not written on
- Dress code: conservative, formal, branded; French executives dress impeccably

**Intellectual Rigor in French Business:**
- French executives expect conceptual depth; PowerPoint decks should have intellectual substance, not just data visuals
- Be prepared to defend your analytical framework — "Quelle est votre logique?" is a challenge, not just a question
- Cartesian reasoning (logical, deductive, from first principles) is the expected thought pattern
- Avoid American-style "let's just try it" pragmatism — French culture requires conceptual clarity before action

**Meeting Protocol:**
- Punctuality: expected for first meetings; social flexibility increases once relationship is established
- Agenda: distributed in advance (Ordre du Jour); deviating from it signals poor preparation
- Debate is healthy and expected; French intellectual tradition values rigorous argument
- Decisions rarely made in meetings — meetings are for discussion; decisions follow offline deliberation
- Lunch meetings are important relationship-building occasions; expect 2-hour lunches with wine

### French Public Procurement (Marchés Publics)

**Framework:**
- Governed by Code de la commande publique (since 2019, consolidating prior Ordonnances)
- UGAP: Union des Groupements d'Achats Publics — purchasing central; qualifying on UGAP framework contracts enables sales to all French public bodies without individual tender
- Above €40,000: published appel d'offres (call for tenders) required
- Above €215,000 (services): full EU procurement procedure (OJEU publication required)

**Response Process:**
1. Veille appels d'offres: monitor BOAMP (Bulletin Officiel des Annonces de Marchés Publics) and platforms like e-marchés publics, AWS (Achat Public)
2. Mémoire technique: detailed technical response document; requires methodological rigor, reference cases, team CVs
3. Cadre de réponse financier: price offer in prescribed format; lowest price is rarely the sole criterion
4. Soutenance: oral presentation to jury — typically the most important differentiator
5. Attribution: notification of award; unsuccessful candidates may request debriefing (rapport de présentation)

**Relationship Building with Public Sector:**
- Direction de la Commande Publique must be respected (they enforce process); but relationships with Direction Métier (operational client) are where need is defined
- Former Énarques and IGF (Inspection Générale des Finances) alumni in public organizations are high-influence; IGF alumni network is disproportionately powerful in Bercy (Ministry of Finance)

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

- **Overly casual communication style:** American informality reads as unprofessional in France. Maintain formal tone, even in email, until the relationship matures to explicit informality.
- **Skipping the conceptual framework:** Jumping to recommendations without presenting the analytical framework first. French executives will not be persuaded by conclusions without rigorous reasoning. Show your method.
- **Ignoring the Grande Écoles network:** Assuming merit and content alone will win mandates. Relationships matter enormously; a warm introduction from a shared "camarade" transforms cold outreach into a real conversation.
- **Misreading debate as rejection:** A French executive who argues vigorously with your analysis is engaged, not dismissing you. Intellectual pushback is part of the process; capitulating immediately signals weakness.
- **Underestimating the public sector complexity:** Appel d'offres processes are legally constrained and technically demanding. Non-compliant bids are disqualified outright. Get the administrative requirements (DC1, DC2 forms) right before focusing on content.
