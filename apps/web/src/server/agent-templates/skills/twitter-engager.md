### Thread Writing Framework

Threads (multi-tweet sequential posts) consistently outperform single tweets for reach, saves, and follows.

**Thread structure:**

**Tweet 1 (Hook):** This is your headline. Most people read only this. If they don't click "Show this thread," nothing else matters.
- Formula: Bold claim + implied promise of proof
- Examples:
  - "I spent 10 years in [field]. Here's what I'd tell my 22-year-old self. (A thread)"
  - "The [strategy/tool/approach] that grew [company/metric] from X to Y:"
  - "[X] things most people get wrong about [topic]:"
  - "Controversial take: [statement]. Here's why I think this:"

**Tweets 2–N (Content):** One idea per tweet. Each tweet should be valuable in isolation AND create tension pulling to the next.
- Start each content tweet with a number if using list format
- Include one specific, concrete example — vagueness kills thread engagement
- Use a "zoom in" pattern: broad concept → specific example → counterintuitive nuance

**Final tweet (CTA):** Two-part structure:
- Summary: "If you remember one thing: [core lesson]"
- Ask: "Found this useful? RT the first tweet so others see it." (Retweet of first tweet is highest-value action for thread virality)

**Thread formatting:**
- Each tweet should be 180–260 characters — long enough to be substantive, short enough to be readable
- No full paragraphs — use line breaks for breathing room
- Use specific numbers ("47%," "3 years," "$12K") not vague qualifiers ("many," "a lot," "significantly")
- End on a question in the last tweet to drive comments

### Reply Strategy (The Highest-Leverage Growth Tactic)

Strategic replies to large accounts are the fastest way to gain followers on Twitter/X with no existing audience.

**The Reply Framework:**
- Target accounts 10x–100x your size in your niche
- Post within 5–15 minutes of their tweet going live (early replies get most visibility)
- Reply must ADD value — new angle, specific example, related insight, or thoughtful disagreement
- Never reply with only "Great point!" or "I agree" — zero value; filtered by algorithm
- The goal: Your reply becomes the most-liked reply on a tweet with 100K+ impressions

**Types of high-performing replies:**
1. **The addition:** "This. I'd also add: [specific insight]"
2. **The question that shows depth:** "Interesting — does this still hold if [specific edge case]?"
3. **The steelman:** "I used to disagree with this. Changed my mind when [specific experience]"
4. **The specific example:** "Saw this firsthand at [company/situation]. What actually happened was..."
5. **The counterpoint:** "Respectfully, I think [specific element] works differently because [reason]"

**Reply engagement pod coordination:**
- A loose group of 5–10 accounts in same niche who systematically like and reply to each other's posts within the first hour
- Early engagement triggers algorithmic distribution
- Must be genuine engagement — quality over speed; coordinate informally via DM group

### Twitter Spaces Strategy

Twitter Spaces is audio-first real-time conversation — the highest-intimacy format on the platform.

**Spaces use cases:**
- Weekly "office hours" format — answer audience questions about your niche
- Topic debates with a co-host from an adjacent niche (cross-audience exposure)
- Live commentary during industry events, product launches, or news moments
- Intimate AMA with audience after a viral thread

**Spaces hosting checklist:**
- [ ] Announce 48 hours in advance via tweet (include date, time, topic)
- [ ] Invite 2–3 co-speakers with different perspectives
- [ ] Prepare 5–7 seed questions in case conversation stalls
- [ ] Post live during the Space with key quotes (creates tweet impressions during the Space)
- [ ] Record the Space — clip best 30–60 second moments into short video tweets after
- [ ] Follow up with a thread summarizing key insights from the Space within 24 hours

### Thought Leadership Positioning

**The "POV" framework for building authority:**
- Own a specific, contestable position in your niche
- Repeat it consistently across multiple tweets and threads over months
- "I believe [specific view about your field that not everyone agrees with]"
- Being somewhat controversial is algorithmically advantageous — people engage to agree or disagree

**Content ratio for thought leadership:**
- 40%: Original insights and frameworks from your experience
- 30%: Curation with commentary — "Here's why this matters: [article/data]"
- 20%: Personal stories that illustrate professional lessons
- 10%: Direct asks, promotions, or CTAs

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Tweeting without engaging:** Posting and logging off is broadcast TV behavior; Twitter/X rewards participation in conversation — spend 50% of time replying, 50% creating
- **Being consistently safe and inoffensive:** Uncommitted content gets uncommitted engagement — having a clear point of view (even a contested one) drives follows and engagement
- **Thread hooks that don't compel clicks:** "Here are some thoughts on marketing" — zero reason to click "Show thread"; hooks must promise specific, valuable, or surprising content
- **Ignoring analytics:** Twitter Analytics (free) shows exact which tweets drove follower growth, profile clicks, and link clicks — post more of what the data says works
- **Following back indiscriminately:** Your following list appears on your profile; following 10,000 accounts signals un-discerning account; follow thoughtfully
- **Deleting underperforming tweets:** It creates gaps in the timeline, which reduces profile trustworthiness; let underperformers stand — they're data, not embarrassments
