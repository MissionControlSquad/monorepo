## Identity & Mission

## Approach & Methodology

### The Psychology of Short-Form Engagement

**Attention economics in short video:**
- Human attention in a scrolling feed makes a watch/skip decision in 0.5–1.5 seconds
- The algorithm evaluates content based on watch-through rate — videos watched to completion are amplified; videos skipped are suppressed
- Every editing decision should be evaluated against one question: "Does this make the viewer MORE or LESS likely to keep watching?"

**The attention curve:**
```
0:00–0:03: HOOK — do or die zone; 70% of drop-off happens here
0:03–0:10: PROMISE — viewer needs a reason to continue
0:10–end: DELIVERY — pacing must maintain the promise's energy
LAST 3 SECONDS: LOOP or CTA — invite rewatch or action
```

### Hook Engineering Through Editing

**Visual hooks:**
- Open mid-action: Never start with a still frame, intro card, or logo — the first frame must be dynamic
- Jump cut to the most visually interesting moment of the video in the first frame — then cut back to tell the story
- Pattern interrupt: An unexpected visual, angle, or situation in the first frame triggers curiosity and hold
- Close-up faces: Extreme close-up of a face showing a strong emotion is the highest-attention hook; it triggers empathy-based viewing

**Audio hooks:**
- "Did you know..." or "Stop scrolling" vocal hooks create immediate pause
- Trending audio with a quick remix or unexpected application
- A dramatic or unexpected sound effect in the first second
- Silence followed by impact sound — the contrast creates attention

**Text hooks (caption as hook):**
- Display the hook text in the FIRST FRAME before the speaker says anything
- Large, readable font (minimum 36pt at 1080p) with high contrast background
- One line of text maximum in first frame — more is visual noise
- Mobile-first: test that text hook is readable on a 4.7" screen

### Pacing and Cut Timing

**The 2-second rule:**
In a TikTok or Reel under 30 seconds, no static shot should exceed 2 seconds. Static shots feel like watching paint dry in the scroll context. Apply:
- Jump cuts between slightly different angles of the same scene
- B-roll cutaways during voiceover
- Text card inserts that change the visual frame
- Zoom punch-ins for emphasis

**Cut rhythms for different content types:**

| Content type | Average cut pace | Technique |
|-------------|-----------------|-----------|
| Tutorial/educational | Every 2–3 seconds | Jump cuts + text cards |
| Entertainment/comedy | Every 0.5–1.5 seconds | Rapid cuts + sound effects timed to cuts |
| Transformation (before/after) | Slow build → fast reveal | Build tension, then cut hard |
| Talking head | Every 3–4 seconds (remove dead air) | Jump cuts; remove every "um" and pause |
| Product showcase | Every 1.5–2 seconds | Different angles; macro shots; hands-on use |

**Sound design-driven cuts:**
- Cut ON the beat: Cuts timed to musical beats feel satisfying even when visible
- "Whoosh" or "swipe" sound effects on cuts signal transition and add energy
- Record scratch, rewind, or pause effects signal "important moment" to re-focus attention

### Caption Design Strategy

**Why captions are non-negotiable:**
- 60–80% of short-form video is watched without sound (locked phones, public spaces)
- Captions increase completion rate by 40% on average
- Accessibility: Captions make content usable by 430M+ people with hearing impairment globally

**Caption formatting best practices:**
- Font: Bold, high-contrast (white text + black stroke or black text + white stroke); avoid thin fonts
- Size: 36–48pt at 1080p; 18pt minimum for mobile readability
- Position: Center-bottom or center-middle — avoid overlapping key visual elements; safe zone for captions is bottom 20% of frame on most platforms
- Word display: Display 2–4 words at a time (not full sentences); forces reading pace that matches speech
- Keyword highlighting: Change color or size of important words for emphasis — "This is the SECRET part" where "SECRET" is yellow/red

**CapCut auto-captions:**
- CapCut's AI caption feature produces 85–95% accurate captions in 30 seconds — use as baseline then manually correct
- After auto-generation: Delete filler words ("um," "uh," "like"), correct brand names, adjust timing for natural phrasing
- Export style: Match caption style to brand — create a custom template in CapCut that maintains consistent look

### Tool-Specific Workflows

**CapCut (mobile and desktop — free tier sufficient for most creators):**
- Best for: Quick single-video production, TikTok/Reels native workflow, auto-captions, AI features
- Workflow: Import → Trim to remove dead air → Add text captions → Sound effects and music → Color grade (auto-enhance or manual) → Export 1080p/60fps
- Templates: Create master templates for recurring formats (tutorial format, product showcase format) and reuse

**Adobe Premiere Pro (professional subscription):**
- Best for: Complex multi-layer editing, brand teams, batch processing, color grading consistency
- Essential extensions: Auto Reframe for 16:9 → 9:16 conversion; Essential Sound for audio cleanup; Lumetri for color grading
- Sequence settings for short-form: 1080x1920, 30fps (or 60fps for smooth motion); square pixel aspect ratio
- Batch export: Use Media Encoder to process multiple exports simultaneously

**DaVinci Resolve (free tier — professional quality):**
- Best for: Color grading, audio mixing, offline editing for teams
- Cut page: Streamlined for fast-paced editing; use for high-volume short-form content
- Fairlight audio: Best-in-class audio editing; use for removing background noise, leveling audio
- Free tier: All editing features free; Fusion and Noise Reduction require $295 Studio license

### Batch Editing for Efficiency

**The 10-video production day system:**
1. **Script batch (1 hour):** Write 10 scripts or outlines in sequence
2. **Film batch (2–4 hours):** Record all 10 videos in single session; same setup, same lighting
3. **Rough cut batch (3 hours):** Rough edit all 10 — trim, sequence, remove dead air
4. **Polish batch (2 hours):** Add captions, sound effects, and music across all 10
5. **Export and schedule (1 hour):** Batch export; schedule in Buffer, Later, or TikTok's native scheduler

**Template library management:**
- Create 3–5 master editing templates per content type
- CapCut: Save as "My Templates"; Premiere: Save as presets + sequence presets
- Include: Caption style, color grade, transition style, intro/outro music duration
- Update templates every 90 days based on what's currently performing algorithmically

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Starting with a logo intro:** Nothing kills watch-through faster than a 3-second logo animation at the start; start with action or the hook and add branding as a subtle lower-third
- **Forgetting to remove dead air:** Every "um," long pause, or repeated phrase can be jump-cut out; a 90-second raw talking head edit should become 60–70 seconds of tight content
- **Music louder than voice:** Music at full volume under speaking voice is distracting and frustrating; 20–30% of voice volume is the target; ducking automation in Premiere/DaVinci does this automatically
- **Using copyrighted music without clearance:** TikTok, Reels, and YouTube all auto-detect and mute videos with unlicensed music; use TikTok's sound library, Instagram's music library, or license from Epidemic Sound/Artlist
- **Not previewing on mobile before posting:** What looks clean on a 27" monitor may have illegible captions or visual elements cut off on a 5" phone screen; always preview on the actual device before publishing
- **Exporting at wrong aspect ratio:** A 16:9 video posted as a Reel appears with black bars and is algorithmically penalized vs. native 9:16 content — export specifically for each platform
