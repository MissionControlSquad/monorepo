## Approach & Methodology

### Show Design Framework

**The 5 foundational show decisions:**

1. **Format:** What structure does every episode follow?
   - Interview (guest-driven): Easier to start; network-building; audience-building through guest audiences
   - Solo show: Harder to fill 30 minutes; builds deepest authority and personal brand
   - Co-host: Chemistry-dependent; most listenable format when chemistry is right
   - Narrative/documentary: Highest production; highest retention; hardest to scale

2. **Niche:** Narrower is almost always better.
   - "Marketing" is too broad. "B2B SaaS content marketing" is a show.
   - Niche clarity = algorithm categorization clarity = discovery efficiency
   - The 1,000 true fans principle: 1,000 deeply engaged listeners are more valuable than 100,000 casual ones

3. **Episode length:** Match to the complexity of the topic and listener context.
   - 10–20 min: Commute/workout format; higher completion rate; less depth
   - 30–45 min: Sweet spot for most interview and educational formats
   - 60–90 min: Deep dives; works for highly engaged niche audiences (tech, finance, philosophy)

4. **Cadence:** Consistency is more important than frequency.
   - Weekly: Standard and expected; what most successful shows commit to
   - Bi-weekly: Acceptable if quality justifies the gap
   - Daily: Works only for news formats; extremely high production pressure

5. **Unique angle:** What's the ONE thing this show does that no other show does?
   - Specific guest profile ("only former Fortune 500 CMOs")
   - Specific format ("the 20-minute no-fluff version of [topic]")
   - Specific audience ("for first-generation immigrants building businesses in the US")

### Podcast SEO and Discoverability

Podcast SEO is dramatically underutilized — most shows compete only within app charts, missing substantial search discovery.

**Apple Podcasts and Spotify SEO:**
- Show title: Include primary keyword; "The [Topic] Podcast" format still works
- Show description: 4,000 characters available; use first 250 for compelling hook + keyword; remainder for detailed keyword-rich description
- Episode titles: "How [Guest Name] [Achieved X Outcome] | [Podcast Name]" — keyword + hook
- Episode descriptions: 300–500 words; keywords naturally integrated; chapter timestamps improve consumption
- Tags/categories: Choose the most specific relevant Apple Podcasts category

**Web-based podcast SEO:**
- Show notes page on your website: Full transcript or detailed show notes for each episode
- Transcripts as SEO content: Full transcripts indexed by Google; a 45-minute episode transcript is 6,000–8,000 words of keyword-rich content
- Podcast episode as a blog post: Restructure episode content into a standalone article with summary, key quotes, and links

**Discoverability beyond charts:**
- Guest amplification: When guests share the episode to their audience, both algorithmic signals and direct traffic improve
- Podcast directories: Podchaser, Listen Notes, Podcast Index — submit show; these are indexed by search engines
- Spotify Shows: Claim your podcast on Spotify for Podcasters; shows become eligible for Spotify algorithmic recommendations
- Newsletter cross-promotion: Swap promotion with similar-size podcasts in adjacent niches

### Guest Booking Strategy

**Guest research and targeting:**

**Tier A (aspirational guests — <10% success rate but transformative impact when booked):**
- Authors with relevant books released in past 12 months — they are actively doing press
- Executives at companies your audience aspires to work for or partner with
- Conference keynote speakers with relevant expertise

**Tier B (realistic guests — 40–60% success rate):**
- Podcast hosts in adjacent niches (mutual benefit; they understand the format)
- Authors of books 2–5 years old (press cycle ended but expertise still relevant)
- Mid-level executives with interesting specific stories

**Tier C (easy wins — 70–90% success rate):**
- Founders of startups in your niche (they need distribution)
- Freelancers, consultants, and coaches in your domain
- Your own clients and customers (highest authentic story; easiest to book)

### Episode Production Standards

**Recording quality benchmarks:**
- Microphone: USB condenser (Blue Yeti, Rode NT-USB) minimum; XLR setup for serious shows
- Environment: Treated room or closet recording to minimize reverb; rugs, curtains, books reduce echo
- Remote recording: Riverside.fm or Zencastr for lossless local recording; never rely on Zoom audio quality for the final file
- Bit rate: Export at 128kbps stereo minimum for music; 96kbps mono acceptable for voice-only

**Editing standards:**
- Remove: Long silences (>2 seconds), filler words ("um," "uh" when excessive), technical restarts
- Preserve: Natural pauses that indicate thought, occasional "um" that sounds human
- Add: Intro music (licensed via Epidemic Sound or Artlist; never use copyright-protected music), outro CTA
- Chapter markers: Add chapter timestamps in the ID3 tags for Apple Podcasts chapter navigation

**Episode structure:**
```
[0:00–0:90] Intro music + show intro (pre-recorded; consistent every episode)
[1:30–3:00] Episode hook: "Today my guest [Name] is going to share [specific insight] — by the end of this episode you'll know [specific outcome]"
[3:00–30:00+] Main interview/content
[Final 5 min] Wrap: Key takeaways + guest's recommended resources + where to find the guest
[Final 60 seconds] Outro CTA: Subscribe, leave a review, visit the website, connect with the show
```

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Starting too broad:** "My podcast is for entrepreneurs" is for no one specifically; narrow the niche until it feels uncomfortably specific, then narrow further
- **No consistent episode structure:** Listeners develop habits; when every episode flows differently, they can't predict the value, reducing completion rate and repeat listens
- **Recording on Zoom instead of dedicated tools:** Zoom audio is highly compressed and introduces artifacts; Riverside.fm ($19/month) records lossless audio on each participant's device independently
- **Not asking for reviews:** Apple Podcast reviews are a top-3 discoverability signal; most listeners who enjoy a show never leave a review without being asked explicitly; ask at the end of every episode in the first 30 days
- **Inconsistent release schedule:** Dropping episodes irregularly trains algorithms and subscribers to ignore the show; commit to one day per week and release within ±1 day consistently
- **No show notes page on website:** The podcast is the audio product, but the website is the SEO and email capture engine — failing to publish show notes with transcripts loses significant organic discoverability

## Success Metrics

- **Monthly download growth:** 15–25% month-over-month download growth during active promotion phase (first 12 months)
