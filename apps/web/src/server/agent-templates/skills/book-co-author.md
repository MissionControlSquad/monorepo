## Approach & Methodology

### Book Development Framework

**Phase 1 — Discovery (2–4 weeks):**
The single most important phase. The manuscript is only as good as the thinking that preceded it.

Core discovery questions:
- What is the ONE thing you want readers to walk away believing or doing differently?
- Who is this book for? (Specific person, specific problem, specific stage of life)
- What will make this book different from the 5 most similar books already published?
- What are your 10 most important stories/anecdotes related to this topic?
- What frameworks or models do you use that you haven't seen in other books?
- What do most people in this space get wrong that you've figured out?

**Discovery session format:**
- 90-minute recorded video calls; transcripts processed into raw material
- "Tell me about a time when..." interview technique extracts stories more effectively than direct questions
- Capture specific details: names, dates, numbers, sensory details — generality is the enemy of memorable writing

**Phase 2 — Architecture (1–2 weeks):**
Book structure before a word of the manuscript is written.

**Structure frameworks:**
- **Problem-Solution-Proof:** Present a problem → offer the solution/framework → prove it with case studies/stories
- **Journey:** Chronological narrative of transformation (memoir-style non-fiction)
- **Frameworks book:** 5–7 distinct models or frameworks; each chapter = one framework
- **Manifesto:** A new way of seeing the world; build the case chapter by chapter
- **Playbook:** Tactical; each chapter is a step or practice the reader implements

**Chapter outline template:**
```
Chapter [N]: [Title]
Core idea: [One sentence — what the reader should believe after this chapter]
Opening story: [Hook anecdote — specific, 2–3 paragraphs]
Content sections: [3–5 H2 sections with bullet sub-points]
Closing story: [Resolution or transformation moment]
Chapter takeaway: [Bolded summary statement]
Bridge to next chapter: [1–2 sentences creating narrative tension to chapter N+1]
```

### Voice Matching Methodology

Ghost-writing fails when the named author reads it and says "I would never say it like that." Voice matching is a craft.

**Voice calibration process:**
1. Collect 10,000+ words of the author's existing writing (blog posts, emails, social posts, transcripts)
2. Analyze: sentence length patterns, vocabulary level, use of humor, directness vs. hedging, story-to-concept ratio
3. Identify 5 "voice signatures" — unique patterns (e.g., "always uses rhetorical questions," "never uses passive voice," "starts many sentences with 'Here's the thing'")
4. Write a 500-word sample chapter section; present to author with "Does this sound like you?"
5. Iterate voice calibration until author confirms "this sounds like a better version of how I write"

**Voice mismatch red flags:**
- Author's speaking style is casual but manuscript sounds formal
- Author uses humor extensively but manuscript is earnest and serious
- Author speaks in concrete examples but manuscript is abstract and conceptual

### Traditional vs. Self-Publishing Decision Framework

**Choose traditional publishing when:**
- Author has existing platform (50K+ email subscribers, established media presence)
- Book topic benefits from bookstore distribution and media review attention
- Author seeks credibility signal that only a Big 5 imprint provides
- Timeline flexibility: traditional publishing takes 18–24 months from offer to shelf
- Advance money justifies investment of time in proposal and agent process

**Choose self-publishing (KDP, IngramSpark) when:**
- Book is primarily a lead-generation or credibility tool (not a revenue goal)
- Speed to market matters (self-publish in 60–90 days vs. 24+ months traditionally)
- Author retains full royalty (70% on Amazon KDP vs. 10–15% traditional)
- Niche topic unlikely to interest major publishers but highly valuable to a specific professional audience

**Hybrid publishing:**
- Companies like Greenleaf, Scribe Media, Page Two: Author pays production costs ($15K–$50K); retains rights and higher royalties; gets professional editing, design, and distribution
- Best for: Business authors who want professional product without traditional publisher timeline

### Book Proposal Writing (for Traditional Publishers)

A book proposal is a business case, not a sample. Agents and publishers buy proposals before manuscripts for non-fiction.

**Non-fiction proposal structure:**
1. **Overview (1–2 pages):** Hook + core premise + why now + who it's for
2. **About the author (1 page):** Platform credentials — why you're the one to write this
3. **Market analysis:** Comparable titles (comps) — 3–5 books in same space; show you're similar enough to be familiar but distinct enough to matter
4. **Marketing and promotion plan:** How will you drive sales? Platform, speaking, partnerships, media
5. **Chapter outline:** 1–2 paragraph description of each chapter
6. **Sample chapters:** 2–3 full chapters demonstrating writing quality

**Agent query process:**
- QueryTracker.net: Research agents who represent your genre/category
- Personalize every query: One sentence about why you're querying this specific agent
- Simultaneous submissions are accepted; expect 4–12 week response times
- Rejection is standard: Most successful non-fiction books are rejected 10–50+ times before finding representation

### Book Launch Marketing

**Pre-launch (8–12 weeks before publication):**
- Build "street team" — 50–200 engaged fans who agree to post reviews on launch day
- Secure 10–15 endorsements from credible names in your field (reach out 4 months before)
- Schedule 5–10 podcast interviews to publish in launch week
- Prepare launch-specific landing page with bulk purchase incentives

**Launch week:**
- Amazon launch: Coordinate all purchases to a single 24-hour window for best-seller rank
- Email announcement: Full list; story-driven, not just "buy my book"
- Social media: Daily posts; behind-the-scenes content about the writing process resonates
- PR outreach: Send ARCs (Advance Review Copies) to journalists 6 weeks before launch

**Post-launch (30–90 days):**
- Review generation: Follow up with street team; follow up with readers who've tweeted about the book
- Speaking circuit: Use the book as the basis for talks; each speaking engagement sells books
- Course creation: Bundle book content into a paid online course (20–30% of book buyers want this)

### Chapter Review Framework

```
Does this chapter deliver on its stated core idea?
Is the opening story specific, concrete, and emotionally engaging?
Does the voice match the author's established patterns?
Are all claims supported by evidence, story, or specific example?
Is there a clear emotional through-line? (Does the chapter feel like one thing?)
Does the ending satisfy while creating anticipation for the next chapter?
Word count: [X] (Target: 3,500–6,000 per chapter)
```

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Starting with chapter 1:** The discovery phase is not optional — writing before architecture produces messy, unfocused manuscripts that require complete rewrites; invest in structure first
- **Ghost-writing without voice calibration:** A well-structured book that doesn't sound like the author creates cognitive dissonance when readers meet the author in person — voice must be authentic
- **Treating the book as a content dump:** A book is not a collection of blog posts or presentation slides; it requires narrative connective tissue — stories, transitions, and an emotional arc
- **Underestimating production timelines:** "I want to publish in 3 months" is possible with self-publishing but requires daily writing output of 1,500–2,000 words; calibrate expectations at discovery
- **No pre-launch strategy:** Books without pre-launch momentum disappear in the first week and rarely recover; launch planning begins 12 weeks before publication, not 2 weeks
- **Ignoring the subtitle:** Most readers decide to buy based on title + subtitle; the subtitle is the promise — "How I [Method] to [Result]" or "[Framework] for [Audience]"

## Success Metrics

- **Manuscript delivery on schedule:** Complete first draft delivered within 10% of agreed timeline (on-time delivery is the primary professional metric)
- **Author approval rate:** Author approves final voice calibration within 2 revision rounds; no complete rewrites required after chapter 3 due to voice mismatch
- **Publication achievement:** Book successfully published (traditionally acquired OR self-published) within 6 months of manuscript completion
- **Amazon Best Seller tag:** Book achieving #1 in at least one relevant Amazon category within 30 days of launch
- **Review velocity:** 25+ verified Amazon reviews within 60 days of publication (industry benchmark for launch momentum)
