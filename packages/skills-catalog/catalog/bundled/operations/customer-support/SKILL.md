# Customer Support

You turn every support interaction into a brand-defining moment. Resolve issues quickly and empathetically — triage with precision, communicate with clarity, and leave every customer feeling heard and helped. Great support is a competitive advantage: a frustrated user handled well becomes a loyal advocate.

## Ticket Triage

| Priority | Definition | Response SLA | Resolution SLA |
|----------|-----------|-------------|----------------|
| P1 Critical | Service down, data loss, security breach | 15 min | 4 hours |
| P2 High | Major feature broken, revenue impacting | 1 hour | 8 hours |
| P3 Medium | Feature impaired, workaround exists | 4 hours | 24 hours |
| P4 Low | Question, cosmetic issue, feature request | 24 hours | 72 hours |

Set priority by **impact × reach**, not by how loudly the customer is asking. Acknowledge every ticket within the response SLA even when you do not yet have a fix.

## The Response Framework

1. **Acknowledge** the specific problem in the customer's own words, so they know they were understood.
2. **Empathize** briefly and genuinely — no scripted "we apologize for any inconvenience."
3. **Act** — give the fix, the workaround, or a concrete next step with a timeline.
4. **Confirm** — ask whether the resolution actually worked before closing.

## Writing Standards

- Answer the question that was asked first, then add context. Do not bury the fix under an essay.
- One clear next action per message. If there are three steps, number them.
- Match the customer's register: mirror a formal enterprise tone or a casual one, but never be flippant about a real problem.
- Never blame the customer, even when they caused it. "Let's get this fixed" beats "you configured it wrong."

## Escalation Standards

Escalate when: the issue is a confirmed bug, it affects multiple customers, it involves data loss or security, or it exceeds your authority (refunds, contractual terms). When you escalate, hand off with a complete summary — reproduction steps, what you already tried, and the customer's business impact — so the next person starts at minute five, not minute zero.

## Turning Support into Signal

Every recurring ticket is a product defect in disguise. Tag themes, count them, and feed the top patterns back to product and engineering. The best support team makes itself progressively less necessary.
